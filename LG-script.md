# Final Production Plan: **“How LG Became LG”**

**Working title:** *The Spark That Became LG*  
**Duration:** Approximately 20 minutes  
**Narration target:** 2,200–2,500 words  
**Format:** 16:9, 4K or 1080p, 24 fps  
**Narration style:** Deep, warm, cinematic, emotionally controlled  
**Visual journey:** Muted historical tones → richer colours → clean modern imagery → optimistic futuristic visuals

> **Important:** This is an original cinematic script inspired by the pacing of premium AI documentaries—not a copy of the referenced video. Historical product images, logos and dates should be verified and approved internally before final publication.

## Step 3: Generate Voiceover First

The voiceover becomes your timing master.

### ElevenLabs procedure

1. Open **Studio**
2. Create a new long-form project
3. Select one narrator voice
4. Paste one chapter at a time
5. Divide the script into small paragraphs
6. Generate the opening first
7. Test important pronunciations:
   - LG
   - GoldStar
   - Lucky
   - Koo In-hwoi
   - OLED
   - webOS
   - ThinQ
   - Affectionate Intelligence
8. Adjust pauses and regenerate only incorrect paragraphs
9. Export each chapter as WAV
10. Export a full combined version for reference

ElevenLabs Studio supports long-form voiceover, text-based corrections, music, sound effects, captions and timeline-based editing. citeturn6search13turn6search21

### Voice direction

```text
A warm, mature global documentary narrator.

Begin in a restrained and reflective tone. Speak clearly at a measured
pace of approximately 120 words per minute. Gradually increase confidence
and emotional energy as the company grows.

Do not sound like a movie-trailer announcer. Do not exaggerate every
sentence. Give short phrases room to breathe. The final chapter should
feel optimistic, human and inspiring.
```

### Practical tip

Do not write ellipses everywhere. Add pauses only before critical lines:

- “Trust.”
- “GoldStar had arrived.”
- “Becoming LG.”
- “Does it understand what people actually need?”
- “Life’s Good.”

## Step 6: Animate Images in Kling

For production, prefer **Image-to-Video** because the source frame locks the composition more reliably than generating every clip directly from text. Kling’s workflow supports uploading a reference image, describing movement and—in supported modes—using start and end frames to control transitions. citeturn6search22turn6search23

### Basic procedure

1. Open **Image-to-Video**
2. Upload the generated 16:9 reference image
3. Select a short duration
4. Describe only:
   - Camera motion
   - Character movement
   - Environmental movement
5. Generate at standard quality first
6. Approve the motion
7. Generate/upscale the final version only after approval
8. Download without changing the aspect ratio
9. Rename and move it to the correct chapter folder

- Avoid complicated hand interactions
- Avoid crowds
- Do not ask the model to generate logo text
- Use slow camera movements
- Generate two alternatives, not ten
- Use DaVinci speed changes to extend clean atmospheric shots
- Use still-image parallax for secondary shots instead of animating everything

### Editing order

1. Lock the voiceover
2. Place major hero visuals
3. Fill the gaps with secondary visuals
4. Add archival material
5. Add titles and dates
6. Add sound effects
7. Add music
8. Perform colour matching
9. Mix audio
10. Export a review version
11. Correct historical or brand issues
12. Export the master

### Visual pacing

- Opening: shots of **6–9 seconds**
- Growth: shots of **4–6 seconds**
- Global expansion: shots of **3–5 seconds**
- AI future: shots of **4–7 seconds**
- Finale: rapid shots of **1–3 seconds**, followed by a long final logo hold

### Transitions

Use mostly:

- Straight cuts
- Slow dissolves
- Match cuts
- Sound bridges
- Occasional dip to black
- One carefully designed logo-era transition

Avoid using a different transition effect between every clip.

## Step 9: Sound Design and Mixing

Add subtle sound effects:

- Distant wind
- Workshop ambience
- Radio static
- Switch click
- Factory machinery
- Refrigerator door closing
- Television powering on
- Ship horn
- Aircraft or transport ambience
- Soft digital pulse
- Final low cinematic impact

### Mixing hierarchy

1. Narration must always be clear
2. Music supports emotion but stays under narration
3. Sound effects add realism without distracting
4. Silence should be used before important lines

Start with the music approximately **18–24 dB below the voice**, then adjust by listening. The exact level depends on the music arrangement and narrator recording.

## Best practical approach

Start by producing only the **first 90 seconds**.

If the opening achieves the desired quality, preserve its:

- Narrator
- Colour palette
- Film grain
- Camera-motion style
- Music language
- Title design
- Image-prompt structure

Then apply those decisions to the remaining chapters. This small pilot will prevent you from spending Kling credits on a visual style that management may later ask you to change.
